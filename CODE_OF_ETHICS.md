# Code of Ethics

Delivery Archaeology exists to understand delivery systems, not to measure individual worth.

The tool works with data that can easily be misused. Its reports should support better conversations about flow, constraints, and trade-offs. They should not become a proxy for blame, surveillance, or performance ranking.

## Principles

1. Measure systems, not people.

   Analysis should focus on work, queues, dependencies, rework, waiting time, review flow, data quality, and delivery variability.

2. Prefer evidence over intuition.

   Findings should be grounded in observable Jira and GitHub records, with sample sizes visible.

3. Separate observation from interpretation.

   A metric can show that blocked work took longer. It does not prove why the blockage happened, who caused it, or whether the delay was avoidable.

4. Use metrics to improve conversations, not to assign blame.

   The intended user is an engineering manager trying to understand where delivery time went and how to explain system health responsibly.

5. Make every finding explainable and reproducible.

   Reports should show enough evidence for another person to understand how the conclusion was reached.

6. Treat data quality as part of the result.

   Missing links, unknown statuses, incomplete histories, and inconsistent workflows are themselves useful evidence about the delivery system.

7. Avoid individual productivity scoring.

   Developer rankings, commit leaderboards, LOC productivity, and story-point comparisons between teams are outside the purpose of this project.

## Responsible Use

Use Delivery Archaeology as a starting point for investigation.

The output can identify issues, pull requests, queues, or workflow states worth reviewing. It should be interpreted with the people who understand the work, the constraints, and the operating context.
